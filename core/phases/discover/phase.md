# Phase 1 — Discover

Discover turns a raw idea, a note, or a half-formed opinion into a validated
content brief: what the piece is, who it is for, why they will care, and what
evidence it can stand on. This is the cheapest phase to be wrong in, so the
work is deliberately skeptical: every claim the post will depend on is either
sourced or marked as an assumption.

## Focus

Topic and audience.

## The AI's role

Research assistant. The model reads, summarises, compares, and drafts
questions. The human owns the judgment: which idea is worth writing, who the
reader is, and what the post must accomplish.

## Outputs

- A content brief with goal, reader, key takeaway, and in/out of scope
- An audience profile: prior knowledge, pain points, reading context
- Research notes with a source register (when the topic needs research)
- A keyword map with search intent (when the post targets search)

## Gates

Every stage in Discover ends at a human approval gate. A stage is not
complete until the human has approved the artifact or requested changes.

## Exit criteria

- The reader and the single key takeaway are explicit
- The scope boundary (what the post will not cover) is written down
- Every load-bearing claim has a source or is flagged as an assumption
- Search intent is known when discoverability matters

## Next phase

When Discover is approved, [Outline](../outline/phase.md) chooses the angle,
builds the structure, and drafts the headline and hook.
